# Curved seated timeline

The current renderer uses smooth, audio-derived holographic ribbons; see [HOLOGRAPHIC_RIBBONS.md](HOLOGRAPHIC_RIBBONS.md). Spatial placement and interaction coordinates below remain unchanged.

Audio-derived waveforms supersede the historical synthesized display and 128-segment geometry budget below. See [AUDIO_WAVEFORMS.md](AUDIO_WAVEFORMS.md) for signed peak caches, 512-column envelopes, gain mapping and stable baseline selection. Arc placement and time direction remain unchanged.

Adjustable zoom now controls the symmetric runtime time window. Normal remains 4 seconds past / 4 seconds future; see [TIMELINE_ZOOM.md](TIMELINE_ZOOM.md) for the authoritative zoom mapping, presets and edit deferral. The historical design past/future fields below remain for compatibility.

The existing RemixTable scene uses a procedural four-lane cylindrical timeline. The XR rig, hand tracking, passthrough, audio clips, automation store, and DSP transport remain in place. RobotArchitect.unity is outside this change.

Defaults in Materials/RemixDesign.asset:
- Radius 0.8 m; horizontal and interactive coverage -90 to +90 degrees. Selection still requires a tracked pinch close to the cylindrical lane baseline.
- Lane centers 0.09 m apart, total band height 0.348 m; center 0.20 m below initial eye height.
- Four seconds of past music and four seconds of upcoming music. The complete arc supports editing.
- 128 segments per lane: 1,032 vertices and 1,024 triangles total, four ribbon renderers sharing the existing material.

The workspace anchors from a valid, settled tracked-camera pose at startup and after a tracking-origin recenter. Its circle center is directly beneath the sampled eye position; it does not follow ordinary head motion. Transport and playhead remain fixed at the forward tangent. All settings are in the RemixDesign asset referenced by the scene manager. Radius, arc angle, interaction angle, arc scale, vertical eye drop, lane spacing, recenter stabilization and past/future seconds are configurable. Segment count is sampled at mesh creation. Apply Curved Layout in Tools/Remix Table saves the layout into the existing scene; the centralized runtime alignment path applies lane spacing and positions the common workspace root.

Mapping: p(theta) = (R sin(theta), laneY + waveformHeight, R(cos(theta)-1)). Hand world positions are transformed into ribbon space; atan2(x,z+R) recovers theta. The legacy LocalX API now returns a normalized angular coordinate expressed in the existing width parameter, rather than a physical flat X position. TimeOffset maps negative angles to the past window and positive angles to the future window. Timestamp adds the shared audio clock modulo song duration. Waveform phase and automation both use that timestamp, so geometry scrolls left through a stationary playhead and remains deterministic at loop boundaries. This extends the inherited synthesized waveform; it is not a sample-accurate PCM waveform display.

Analytical selection measures radial and vertical distance to the stable cylindrical lane baseline, limits acquisition to the configured interaction angle and lane height, and uses no MeshCollider. The full arc has equal base brightness. The raw hand angle rejects points beyond the actual endpoints before clamped coordinates are used for timestamps. Two-hand resizing uses angular coordinates clamped at the arc endpoints, preserving endpoint order across the song seam. One-hand drag uses local vertical displacement. Edit selection synchronously captures the audio clock after the existing global pause owner is acquired; all ribbons read that same frozen clock. Region timestamps remain song-relative. Release relinquishes the existing owner; manual pause continues to take precedence. Gain is stored through the existing automation store and evaluated by both rendering and audio.

## Tracked workspace alignment and recentering

The previous manager placed the workspace once after a one-second timer, without checking HMD tracking validity, then latched `anchored`. It never handled tracking-origin updates. A system recenter could therefore change the tracked camera frame while leaving the world-space workspace at its previous pose. The initial placement formula itself already used camera world space; the fix addresses the missing lifecycle and pose-settling logic rather than adding another positional offset.

`RemixTableManager` is the sole runtime alignment owner. The scene explicitly references the existing rig's Main Camera. Given its actual world position `eye` and rotation, alignment computes `forward = normalize(ProjectOnPlane(camera.forward, worldUp))`, then sets `workspace.position = eye + forward * distance - worldUp * workspaceEyeDrop` and `workspace.rotation = LookRotation(forward, worldUp)`. For the curved workspace, distance is `arcRadius * arcScale` (0.8 m), so local `(0,0,-Radius)` lands beneath the headset and the forward playhead stays one radius ahead. The flat layout continues to use `workspaceDistance` (0.62 m). The configured eye drop remains 0.20 m; with 9 cm lane spacing, top and bottom lane centers sit 6.5 cm and 33.5 cm below the sampled eyes. No floor height or Camera Offset height is added to the camera world pose a second time.

Alignment is requested by the running `XRInputSubsystem.trackingOriginUpdated` event. Quest Android also polls the installed Meta SDK's `OVRPlugin.GetLocalTrackingSpaceRecenterCount`, the same counter used by that SDK's `OVRDisplay`, without creating an OVRManager or XR rig. Changed tracking-origin mode or local Camera Offset pose provides a second fallback for origin/height changes. Rig locomotion, boundary notifications, ordinary camera movement, and even large head-pose jumps alone never request alignment. Providers without an event or usable native counter cannot reliably distinguish an otherwise invisible reference-space reset from real head motion; no jump heuristic is used.

After a request, HMD position and rotation tracking must both be valid. Alignment waits for a stable candidate pose for `recenterStabilizationSeconds` (0.35 s); a change beyond `recenterPositionTolerance` (1.5 cm at the workspace pivot) or `recenterYawTolerance` (2 degrees) restarts the wait. Tracking loss also restarts it. Near-vertical or non-finite head poses are rejected. Ordinary movement after settling leaves the anchor fixed. Unity Editor previews without a running XR subsystem use the scene camera solely for desktop testing; Quest builds require actual tracked HMD input.

Recenter requests cancel gain/filter/region/zoom gestures and release ribbon editing owners before moving the root. Selection is blocked while pending, and interrupted holds require both tracked pinches to release before reacquisition. Automation records, song-relative regions, song selection and zoom settings are retained; manual pause remains authoritative. Ribbons, playhead, transport, mode selectors and zoom feedback remain under the same workspace root. The palm menu stays in its existing hand-relative hierarchy. No Android/OpenXR settings, XR objects, audio assets or RobotArchitect scene are changed.
